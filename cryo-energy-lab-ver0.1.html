01 PUE & facility overhead
Assumptions
PUE & facility overhead

PUE = total facility electricity ÷ IT electricity. The RT baseline is IT × PUE; every chart is normalized to this total.

The presets are 1.54 (survey reference) and 1.09 (Google fleet reference). They are comparison baselines, not AI-specific power breakdowns.

Non-cooling facility power is an editable allowance. We subtract it from total overhead to infer RT cooling. During conversion, this RT cooling is replaced by the selected cold-cooling model plus cooling for remaining warm loads.

Source: Google data-center efficiency. The non-cooling split and warm-load scaling are assumptions of this app.

Why 5 units?

An editable allowance, not a measured constant. It uses the same power units as the IT budgets.

It covers UPS, transformer and PDU losses, plus lighting, security and building controls. Exclude cooling equipment and losses already counted in IT.

PUE includes both cooling and non-cooling overhead, so this input belongs in section 01.

RT cooling = (PUE − 1) × IT power − non-cooling facility power

02 IT power allocation
Assumptions
IT allocation & component shares

Power budgets share one scale; they do not need to sum to 100. Entering kW makes all power results kW.

The accelerator budget includes GPU logic and HBM. The host budget includes CPU and DDR. Defaults split these 80:20 and 90:10 respectively; avoid counting memory twice.

The 55 / 10 / 8 / 10 budgets are illustrative scenario inputs, not a measured universal AI data-center breakdown. Other server power covers supporting electronics and losses included within the IT boundary.

Network, storage and other server loads stay warm in this model. Their remaining-power factors are independent assumptions. Facility overhead is separate.

Share references: H100/H200 power-capping study, Fig. 9 and LLM inference study, Fig. 4 (CPU 182 W, DRAM 20.3 W). These motivate approximate, workload-dependent splits.

03 Temperature & heat
Assumptions
Component temperatures & bath heat

Default operating points: CPU logic 100 K; HBM 100 K; GPU logic 150 K; DDR DRAM 77 K. These components share one 77 K bath.

The component temperatures are scenario assumptions, not predictions from package thermal resistance. When you edit the bath, junctions at the previous bath follow it and any colder junction is raised to the new bath temperature; warmer junction settings stay unchanged. Power ratios remain fixed unless you edit them. Power factors are entered separately in section 04. Changing a junction temperature alone does not interpolate power; changing the bath or heat sink does update refrigeration.

Bath heat = remaining GPU + HBM + CPU + DDR electrical power + parasitic heat. Warmer junctions do not change the refrigerator’s cold boundary from the bath temperature.

Other loads
Assumptions
Other loads during cryogenic operation

These inputs set the power that remains outside the cold-chip group after conversion. Each remaining percentage multiplies that category’s RT budget from sections 01 and 02. A default of 100% assumes no change.

Network/storage and other server electronics stay warm. Additional auxiliaries add both electricity and warm heat. Their heat receives conventional cooling, with the open-loop nitrogen exhaust credit capped by available warm heat.

Non-cooling facility power remains a separate electrical allowance. It is included in the facility total and receives no nitrogen heat-recovery credit in this model.

These are editable scenario assumptions. The default adds no auxiliary power and assumes no savings in these loads.

05 Cooling models
Assumptions
Cooling comparisons & boundaries

Each chip design is evaluated with five cooling settings: open-loop Anhui / Chuzhou, closed-loop LN₂, 50% Carnot, 100% Carnot and the TSMC scenario. The closed-loop and two comparison Carnot efficiencies are independently editable.

All models receive the same bath heat. The RT cooling overhead is replaced; it is not added again. Remaining warm IT and auxiliaries use a cooling factor inferred from the RT PUE.

Only the open-loop Anhui reference credits external nitrogen-exhaust cooling of warm loads, capped at available warm heat. Closed-loop LN₂ retains or recuperates that cold internally, so it receives no second sensible-heat credit. Neither case subtracts a guessed air-separation cost.

Open the question marks beside each model for its equations, input provenance and limitations. These comparisons assume equal throughput and the same system boundary.

Carnot cooling model
W / Q = (T_sink / T_bath − 1) / η

Use kelvin and η as a fraction. 100% Carnot is the reversible limit; 50% Carnot requires twice its work for the same temperatures and bath heat.

This is refrigeration between the bath and sink, not a calculation at the warmer chip junction. Both temperature controls affect this model.

Basis: the standard Carnot refrigerator relation. Efficiency settings are scenario assumptions, not measured refrigerator performance.

Open-loop LN₂: Anhui / Chuzhou reference

The retained 0.60 kWh/L figure covers nitrogen production from air, including air separation and liquefaction, as confirmed by the user. Its separate process-energy shares are not available. It is an open-loop production input, not a measured closed-loop efficiency.

Bath duty sets liquid flow through 160.5 kJ/L latent heat. Delivery yield accounts for lost liquid. Vapor can then cool warm equipment while warming toward the heat sink, with 187.2 kJ/L sensible capacity from 77 to 300 K. External recovery is capped by available warm heat.

The displayed effective Carnot percentage uses only 77 K bath duty divided by production electricity. It is not the exergy efficiency of the whole multi-temperature plant.

Boundary confirmed by the user: air separation is included in 0.60 kWh/L. A closed inventory avoids ongoing fresh-air separation except for makeup; this is already reflected by using a refrigerator-efficiency model instead of a fresh-liquid production cost. The unknown separation share prevents assigning a separate numerical discount.

Closed-loop LN₂ estimate
W_closed = Q_bath × (T_sink / T_bath − 1) / η_closed

Default: 30% of Carnot; explore 20–40%. Nitrogen stays in a closed inventory with recondensation or internal return-stream recuperation. The refrigerator can use a separate working fluid. The user-confirmed open-loop production figure includes air separation. No ongoing fresh-air separation is required for the closed circulating inventory after initial filling; makeup is assumed zero. Avoided separation is already inside this system-boundary change, not an extra subtraction from refrigerator electricity.

This is a net wall-plug estimate: compressor, expander, motor, heat-exchanger approach and ordinary package losses are represented together by η. Initial fill/cooldown and nitrogen makeup are excluded; makeup is assumed zero. Additional facility auxiliaries and bath heat leaks remain editable in sections 04 and 03.

Cold used inside a recuperator cannot also be credited to external warm loads. Capturing 300 K exhaust and reliquefying it is a different case: that gas must be cooled again. This default models retained/recuperated cold, not warm-gas capture alone.

Jin et al. (2018) modeled nitrogen cycles with 24.8% and 32.8% Carnot efficiency near 78 K. We infer a 30% central engineering scenario, not a measured whole-plant result. The 20–40% bracket explores conservative to optimistic assumptions; 40% is not established by that paper. NIST describes return-gas recuperation and less efficient small cryocoolers.

TSMC fitted scenario
f(T) = 0.2 + 0.006938589587 × (350 − T)
W_cooling = Q_bath × f(T)

T is the bath temperature in kelvin. The recovered fit covers 1–350 K and gives 2.094235 W/W at 77 K. Manual mode uses your fixed coefficient instead.

The recovered fit specifies no heat-sink dependence, so changing the sink does not change its cold-cooling factor. Below-Carnot values are flagged as hypothetical.

Equations, model boundaries & sources

The selected design uses CPU logic and HBM at 100 K, GPU logic at 150 K, and DDR DRAM at 77 K. All discharge heat into one 77 K bath. A second design can be enabled in section 03 for comparison. Junction temperatures identify operating points; power ratios remain independent editable inputs because these literature points do not establish a continuous power–temperature curve. Refrigeration uses the bath temperature.

ITRT = accelerator + host + network/storage + other server
PRT = PUE × ITRT
CRT = (PUE − 1) × ITRT − facilityRT
cwarm = CRT / ITRT
Qbath = GPU + HBM + CPU + DDR + parasitic bath heat
Pcryo = cold-chip power + warm IT + facility + auxiliaries + Wcold + cwarm × (Qwarm − Qrecovered)
  • Carnot: Wcold = Qbath × (Tsink/Tbath − 1) / η. The two pies default to η = 100% (reversible limit) and η = 50%. Each efficiency is editable independently.
  • Closed-loop LN₂: the nitrogen inventory is retained and recondensed, or refrigerated through a closed recuperative cycle. W = Q_bath × (T_sink/T_bath − 1) / η_closed. Default η_closed = 30%; sensitivity 20–40%. This is a net wall-plug engineering estimate, including ordinary refrigeration-package losses, not a measured plant specification. Internal recuperation is already represented in η_closed. There is no additional external exhaust-cooling credit. Initial fill and cooldown are excluded from this steady-state balance; nitrogen leakage/makeup is assumed zero.
  • Anhui / Chuzhou: delivered nitrogen flow = Qbath/L. Production flow divides by the delivery yield. Plant electricity = production flow × e × 3600. The exhaust follows the heat sink by default. A manual target is bounded to the bath–sink interval. Sensible capacity S = Cp,vol × (Texhaust,effective − Tbath). Recovered warm heat = min(Qwarm, utilization × delivered flow × S). The electrical credit is cwarm × recovered heat.
  • TSMC “magic”: Wcold = Qbath × f(Tbath). The default recovered fit is f(T) = 0.2 + 0.006938589587 × (350 − T) W/W. It reproduces our saved carnot_vs_tsmc_curve.csv table over 1–350 K (77 K: 2.094235; 100 K: 1.934647; 150 K: 1.587718). No extrapolation beyond this range is applied; choose manual mode to supply another coefficient. This is our prior scenario fit, not a verified universal TSMC law. No heat-sink dependence is specified. Below-Carnot values are labeled as hypothetical.

For the open-loop reference, the default nitrogen properties are L = 160.5 kJ/L and Cp,vol = 187.2/(300−77) kJ/(L·K). This constant heat-capacity approximation is a scenario model. In the open-loop reference, saturated vapor leaves the bath and warms against downstream equipment. In the closed-loop estimate, cold is retained or recuperated internally; no consumable-liquid production electricity or free external sensible cooling is added. Review phase, pressure and property assumptions when changing the bath temperature.

Default RT budgets are 55 accelerator, 10 host, 8 network/storage, 10 other server, and 5 non-cooling facility units. Their common scale is arbitrary. If entered as kW, all absolute results are kW and nitrogen flow is L/s. The overhead split is assumed: PUE specifies only total facility overhead. The 5-unit non-cooling allowance represents distribution losses (UPS, transformers and PDUs) plus lighting, security and building controls; it excludes cooling equipment and anything already counted in IT. Section 01 subtracts this allowance from (PUE − 1) × IT to infer conventional RT cooling, avoiding double counting. Non-cooling facility power is held fixed unless its cryogenic remaining factor is changed.

Default power remaining is GPU 24.9% at 150 K, CPU and HBM 6.2% at 100 K, and DDR approximately 6.96% at 77 K. The 100 K and 150 K points are approximate chip-only ratios retained from the supplied literature plot. Applying them to HBM and GPU is an explicit modelling transfer, not a measured HBM or GPU result. DDR uses NRFC-DRAM with CryoGuard: (100 − 25.9)% / (1 + 9.65) = 6.9577% electronic power, including protection circuitry. The paper’s cooling is removed before the app applies its own cooling models. Optional partition mode replaces the direct GPU/memory factors with weighted RT partitions; it does not reproduce the literature defaults.

No measured performance gain is assumed. Warm auxiliaries are counted as electricity and as warm heat. Facility overhead is a separate lumped allowance. Parasitic bath heat adds refrigeration duty, not IT electricity. Delivery losses increase plant input and do not create an additional usable exhaust credit.
